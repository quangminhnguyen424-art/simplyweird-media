# Research: the history of Tokyo's subway

## Demand check
- The B1M "Tokyo has built the world's best subway" (YouTube, 2021) and several long "history of the Tokyo Metro" videos (11-13 min), plus map-evolution videos (1927-2021). Strong evergreen interest; most are long-form.
Our angle: a 90-second Short told as a story about one person, opening on the bean-counting traffic survey (a vivid, little-known detail), ending on the first line's 100th birthday on Dec 30, 2027 (a reason to watch now). We do not reuse their scripts, maps or footage.

## Facts used (with sources)
- Tokyo Metro + Toei together carry over 8 million passengers a day; Tokyo Metro alone averages 7.04 million a day (FY2025). Sources: Tokyo subway (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_subway), Tokyo Metro business situation (https://www.tokyometro.jp/lang_en/corporate/enterprise/transportation/conditions/index.html)
- Hayakawa visited London in 1914, saw the Underground and concluded Tokyo needed one; trams in Tokyo were congested. Sources: Ginza Line (https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Ginza_Line), Noritsugu Hayakawa (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noritsugu_Hayakawa)
- Bean count: white bean in left pocket for each pedestrian, black bean in right pocket for each car. Source: Noritsugu Hayakawa (Wikipedia, citing The Japan Times)
- Founded Tokyo Underground Railway Co. in 1920; post-1923 Great Kanto Earthquake recession delayed construction until 1925. Sources: Old Tokyo (https://www.oldtokyo.com/?p=31585), Tokyo Metropolitan Government urban planning PDF (https://www.toshiseibi.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/keikaku_chousa_singikai/pdf/tokyotoshizukuri/en_2_05.pdf; this one says 1924, most others say 1925, so the script says 1925)
- The original plan was longer (Ueno-Shimbashi); the first section was only 2.2 km, Ueno to Asakusa. Sources: Tokyo Metropolitan Government PDF, Tokyo subway (Wikipedia)
- Opened Dec 30, 1927, publicised as the first subway in the Orient / Asia. Sources: Tokyo Metro news (https://www.tokyometro.jp/lang_en/news/224586.html), Ginza Line (Wikiwand)
- Over 40,000 passengers in the morning hours of opening day. Source: Tokyo Metropolitan Government PDF
- Waits of more than two hours. Source: Ginza Line (Wikiwand)
- Extended to Shimbashi 1934; Shibuya section 1938-1939, through service Asakusa-Shibuya 1939. Source: Tokyo Metropolitan Government PDF
- Marunouchi Line 1954 was the first post-war line; Toei's first line 1960. Source: Tokyo subway (Wikipedia)
- Today: 13 lines, 286 stations, 304 km (combined network, 2023). Source: Tokyo subway (Wikipedia)
- Roppongi (Oedo Line) platform is about 42 m deep, the deepest in Tokyo. Sources: Tokyo Metropolitan Government brochure (https://www.english.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/documents/d/english/about_tokyo_documents_tokyo_brochure_14_1-pdf), Roppongi Station (Wikipedia)
- The Ueno-Asakusa section turns 100 on Dec 30, 2027. Source: Tokyo Metro news (above)
