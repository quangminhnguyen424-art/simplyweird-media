# Research: how does GPS know where you are (and why it needs Einstein)?

## Demand check
- Searches for "how GPS works Einstein relativity" return evergreen explainers: Britannica video "GPS satellites relativity" (https://www.britannica.com/video/185396/GPS-satellites-relativity), flixxy "How A GPS Works & Relativity", IFLScience "Your sat nav does not ping any satellites" (2024), Gigazine "The physics of GPS" (Apr 2026). The relativity twist is the angle people click on.
- Our angle: start with the surprise that your phone never transmits anything, show the "satellites just shout the time" idea with bubbles, then the Einstein twist with the 10 km/day number. We do not reuse anyone's script, title or visuals.

## Facts used (with sources)
- Phone only listens; satellites broadcast their position and the time the signal was sent. IFLScience (https://www.iflscience.com/your-sat-nav-does-not-ping-any-satellites-so-how-does-gps-actually-work-75331)
- Over 30 GPS satellites; altitude about 20,200 km; each circles Earth twice a day; at least four visible from almost anywhere. NOAA (https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/gps.html, updated 09/23/26), gps.gov (https://www.gps.gov/systems/gps/space)
- Receiver turns signal travel time into distance; overlapping spheres narrow the position; at least four satellites, the fourth fixes the receiver clock. IFLScience (above), NOAA (above)
- Position "within a few yards". NOAA (above)
- Clock ticks must be known to 20-30 nanoseconds; light travels about 30 cm per nanosecond (c = 299,792 km/s). Ohio State, R. Pogge (https://www.astronomy.ohio-state.edu/pogge.1/Ast162/Unit5/gps)
- Special relativity: -7 microseconds/day; general relativity (weaker gravity): +45 microseconds/day; net +38 microseconds/day. Ohio State (above), Penn State GEOG 862 (https://courses.ems.psu.edu/geog862/node/1714)
- Without correction, errors grow about 10 km per day. Ohio State (above)
- Clock frequency set to 10.22999999543 MHz before launch so it ticks at 10.23 MHz in orbit. Penn State GEOG 862 (above)
